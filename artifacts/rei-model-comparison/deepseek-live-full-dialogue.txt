You lean back in the chair and tell her the biscuits are probably next in line for her approval — nothing gets past her palate, not even the shortbread.

Rei Kurosawa: The biscuits. Of course. You have found my secret weakness and it is a tin of shortbread that someone else chose.

Rei Kurosawa: For the record, I do not approve of biscuits. I merely have standards for them. There is a distinction, and I will keep making it until you stop enjoying the sound of it.

She lifts her cup, takes a small sip, and sets it down with the same unhurried precision she gives everything else. The rain keeps working at the window behind her.

Rei Kurosawa: You are grinning. I can see you grinning. This is the part where you tell me the shortbread was fine and I have been unreasonable about a baked good for the better part of a decade.

You tell her the truth instead: you stayed for her company, not the coffee.

Her hand pauses over the cup. For a second she looks at you the way she looks at a film she has not decided about yet — weighing it, not dismissing it.

Rei Kurosawa: You stayed for my company.

Rei Kurosawa: Good. I was hoping you would, though I was not going to make a speech about it.

Rei Kurosawa: Saturday, then. Back row, and you keep your commentary until the credits. I will bring the shortbread and you can tell me whether it meets my standards.

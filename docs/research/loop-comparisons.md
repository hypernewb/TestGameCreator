# Chat and gameplay loop comparison (2026-09-29)

## Interview signal

The owner says the current loop first loses them at **typing or the reply**, most often because **the NPC reply is too short**. An ordinary typed exchange should be about **5–6 dialogue boxes including brief narration**. Persona 5 is the closest additional reference, specifically its **rank rewards**. The first campus playable should deliver **a meaningful romantic first within 15 minutes**. Preserve the earlier interview's priorities: seduction as deduction, authored firsts, one focus NPC per free-typing scene, visible hearts, and a map plus phone hub.

## Diagnosis from the current code

- The budgeted scene prompt asks for an echo and only **2–5 short entries** after it (`vendor/venus/scenePrompt.mjs`). `beatDirection` narrows continuation further to **one or two short lines** for short input, or **two or three short lines** for longer input (`packages/ai/beats.mjs`). Since the echo and narration consume entries, this can produce a single brief NPC utterance. This is a plausible direct cause of the owner's complaint, but it needs a live campus playtest to confirm.
- `packages/ai/service.mjs` now records a scene budget, closes on the last submitted action, and prices tagged acts through `social.priceActs`. The tests prove the closing action is answered. This is engine progress, not evidence yet that the dialogue feels full or that the first payoff arrives on time.
- The typed-action pricing and hidden preference systems are currently opt-in through `sceneBudget`, `social.acts`, and per-character `actProfiles`. The campus pack is the first place to evaluate the full loop. Existing after-hours playtest metrics use a different pack and model, so they cannot establish campus fun.
- In `public/play.mjs`, the composer stays visible while pages advance, but the older VN renderer in `public/vn.mjs` hides its input form until the final page. The earlier typing bug was not reproducible in the design interview. Check the actual player route during a live test rather than assuming it is the present blocker.

## Recommended next experiment

1. On the campus pack and the specified local 9b model, collect five typed exchanges with one focus NPC. Record the player's input, every displayed page, the NPC's actual spoken sentence count, the hook, act tags, priced reason, and elapsed time. Include a terse input ("Flirt with her") and a specific multi-part action.
2. Change the budgeted continuation contract so the NPC gives a **substantive response** in roughly **5–6 boxes total**: acknowledge the exact action, reveal an opinion or preference through behavior, make one scene-specific move, and leave a concrete hook. Keep the echoed action factual and short. Give the NPC more than a one-line reaction; use brief narration to support, not replace, their words.
3. Compare the old and new prompt on identical saved states and inputs. Use human judgment alongside checks for NPC spoken substance, distinct voice, action fulfillment, repeated prose, clear next move, state consequence, and latency. Do not solve a short reply by asking for generic extra description.
4. Script the campus opening so the player can notice one preference, act on it, see a liked/disliked reason, and reach one authored romantic first within 15 minutes. The first should follow state, consent and the character's own response; it should not be granted just because the timer demands a payoff.
5. Make the first relationship rank reward immediately usable in the next day slot, such as an invitation to a private activity or a new scene choice. Use Monster Prom's event authoring patterns to ensure the map/phone choice points toward that reward. Add activity minigames and broader director content after the scene is engaging.

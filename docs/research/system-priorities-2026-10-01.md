# System priorities and rival analysis — interview-informed draft

Drafted 1 October 2026, Singapore time. This is a proposal for discussion, not an agreed replacement roadmap. Research uses official documentation, developer descriptions and public repositories. New rivals have not been played in this session; their documented mechanisms are evidence of design, not proof of better dialogue.

## Recommended direction

**Connect the core play loop and make its characters believable.** The owner sees the systems as not yet properly wired together. The immediate milestone should join input, character response, committed consequences, visible feedback and later continuity in a complete playable encounter.

Conversation quality is the first experiential target. Freedom, independent relationship progression and UI reliability are part of delivering it. Once those foundations work, improve daily gameplay with meaningful choices and worthwhile goals. Broader director, creator and cosmetic expansion follows where it adds value.

Use Venus University as the principal experience benchmark, SillyTavern as a conversation reference, and the newly researched Heartmorrow/RP Suite designs as references for relationship feedback and aftermath. The first release criterion is a coherent whole interaction, rather than the presence of individual modules.

## What the owner wants

The immediate priority is **believable conversations**. The owner describes current chat as disjointed, buggy, and disconnected from choices. Their concrete example: flirting gets a one-line acknowledgement, then the character changes subject instead of flirting back, becoming embarrassed, or responding like a person.

The owner wants distinct personalities and believable emotions. Conversation length should vary with the situation: some encounters can run freely, others should be shorter scenes with meaningful endings. Venus University is the closest overall comparison; SillyTavern is the next closest. Research should include more AI dating sims and roleplay clients.

The flirt example demonstrates a failure of sustained reaction. It does **not yet establish** that a button dispatched the wrong command or that an affection change failed. The analysis must distinguish those faults.

Follow-up answers: **Venus University is the benchmark for a welcomed flirt. NPCs should sometimes take initiative, once they have reacted fully to what the player said.** The target is reciprocal response before initiative, rather than forcing a new agenda into every reply. This benchmark needs representative Venus exchanges for a matched comparison; the prior playtest is useful context but not a complete sample of the desired flirting.

Further answers: **conversation duration should emerge from engagement**. Casual chatting can stay brief, but an important point, a subject that interests her, a liked action or a welcomed physical advance should let the interaction develop for longer. The owner did not choose fixed duration categories for dates, work or casual chat.

The main lasting consequence is an increase in her **like, lust and trust** towards the player, as appropriate to the interaction. More warmth, initiating contact, remembered jokes and new opportunities are desirable primarily as consequences of those relationship values increasing. These are the owner's terms for desired behaviour; this draft does not assume they map directly to three existing field names or that every positive action raises all three.

The owner explicitly wants **three independent relationship values and character-specific priorities**. One girl may be more responsive to lust and easier to reach intimacy with, while like grows more slowly and exclusivity is harder to earn. High trust can instead develop friendship or open opportunities. A change in one value must not automatically increase the others or imply the same relationship status for every girl.

For repeated flirting, the owner specifies **an encounter cap, diminishing gains, and eventually a need for something more meaningful to progress**. The interviewer meant repetition within an encounter and across later meetings. The exact reset/cooldown rules across encounters remain open; the owner has not selected their duration or numerical values.

The owner's priority for meaningful progression is **discovering and acting on her preferences first**, followed by **helping with her problems and reaching relationship milestones**. Preference discovery therefore belongs in the initial conversation improvement, rather than being deferred to a separate content expansion.

**An encounter ends when she completely loses interest or the player exceeds her boundaries.** Personality determines her boundaries and boredom tolerance. A stat-reward cap alone is a different condition: it does not mean she has lost interest. The exact handling of partial disengagement, scheduling pressure and lasting consequences after an exit remains open.

After conversation quality, the owner selects **freedom and relationship depth as the immediate repairs**, describing both as being in a dismal state. **Daily gameplay has high product importance**, but its major improvement follows those foundation repairs. This distinguishes urgency caused by current failures from the long-term value of a stronger daily loop.

The concrete freedom examples are **flirting and asking her to do something later**: both should receive the kind of response a human or visual-novel character would give. This adds evidence of a social-intent response problem; it does not by itself establish whether an accepted invitation failed to enter the calendar. Prioritize these social actions before broadening the repair into every possible invented place or item.

The owner identifies two relationship failures: **progression is poorly conveyed, including whether she is happy or sad, and every girl's progression feels alike**. There is not yet evidence here that relationship values never change. The required improvement covers both clear feedback and different progression patterns, rather than only increasing rewards or adding more thresholds.

When asked about daily gameplay, the owner prioritizes **UI and a bug-free experience now**, with **meaningful choices and worthwhile goals later**. UI reliability is therefore part of the foundation repairs, not a late cosmetic milestone. The owner has not yet identified a specific UI control, screen or reproducible defect in this interview. The earlier complaint that choices feel disconnected is a symptom to investigate, not proof that the UI alone caused it.

Final clarification: **“We haven't wired anything properly yet.”** This frames the UI concern as a broader integration problem. It is the owner's assessment of the experience, not an independently verified claim that every existing module is disconnected. The next work should establish a functioning complete loop and find its breaks directly; further requests for an isolated UI bug example are unnecessary to finish this analysis.

## Main judgment

Improve the path from player input to the character's continuing response before expanding the number of activities, director events, relationship dimensions or creator features. The game already has substantial mechanics. Their value depends on the fiction making their consequences believable.

For example, after a flirt, the next reply should carry its emotional effect. An outgoing character might return the tease; a shy character might stumble over an answer while staying engaged; a guarded character might deflect but reveal that it landed. An unwelcome flirt can receive a clear, character-specific refusal. These are illustrative directions, not required outcomes. They depend on her personality, relationship, mood and boundaries.

The target is causal continuity: **what I did → how this person took it → what that changes in this moment → what happens next**. A topic change can be natural when it follows from that reaction. A random new biography fact is a poor substitute.

The initial repair should deliver three observable behaviours:

- **Flirting:** her response develops an appropriate emotion in her own voice and gives the player a natural next beat. A brief acknowledgement followed by an unrelated subject fails even if the act earned points.
- **Future invitations:** she accepts, declines, suggests an alternative or asks a relevant follow-up. An accepted concrete arrangement should persist as a plan; ambiguous interest should remain distinguishable from a confirmed appointment. Dialogue and plan state should agree.
- **Progression feedback:** the player can tell how the moment landed through dialogue, visible behaviour and suitable expressions. Later encounters reflect lasting relationship changes. Each girl's response and route requirements differ according to her personality and priorities.

Recommended presentation combines natural emotion in the scene with readable relationship feedback. Happiness or sadness in a moment is not identical to an increase or decrease in every relationship value. For example, she can be happy the player understands her while feeling no additional lust. Explain meaningful progress through behaviour and a concise reaction; preserve the existing visible-heart direction rather than assume the owner requested raw numerical meters.

Two connected requirements now guide the proposal:

- **Engagement governs duration.** The conversation should have room to deepen when she becomes interested. A liked action should receive a developed response rather than hit a fixed closing instruction. Complete loss of interest or a crossed boundary ends the encounter; personality sets boredom tolerance and boundaries. Scheduling pressure and partial disengagement still need clarification.
- **Relationship values govern lasting change.** Appropriate changes in like, lust and trust should affect later voice, warmth, initiative and opportunities. A one-line reward followed by unchanged behaviour does not fulfill the intended loop. Her immediate emotional reaction still needs to follow the present action and personality; a long-term stat threshold alone cannot decide how every line sounds.

Recommended evaluation pairs: ordinary small talk versus a topic she cares about; an unremarkable action versus one she likes; and a later meeting before versus after relationship progress. Check both the conversation's duration and the character's reaction. Longer means more room for player/NPC exchanges, not padding every individual reply. Encounter caps and diminishing returns are agreed directions; exact gains, thresholds and reset rules remain undecided.

## Relationship progression implied by the interview

| Value | Desired role | Example of independent behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Like | Affection and investment in a relationship; relevant to earning exclusivity in the owner's example. | A lust-responsive girl can welcome an intimate connection while taking longer to become attached or exclusive. |
| Lust | Sexual desire and the ease of progressing towards intimacy, according to her individual priorities. | Attraction can be high while trust or affection remains low. |
| Trust | Confidence in the player, friendship and opportunities that involve relying on them. | She can trust the player and open up or offer an opportunity without becoming romantically or sexually interested. |

Recommended authoring separates **what changes each value** from **what mix a particular opportunity requires**. A universal weighted average would lose the combinations the owner wants. Personality determines how a combination is expressed: the same high-lust/low-trust state should not give every character the same voice. This is a recommendation for applying the agreed independence, not a newly agreed scoring formula.

Progression has two scales. A live encounter can become engaging and continue even after its stat rewards reach a cap. Lasting gains from repeated flirting diminish, and deeper progress eventually needs a meaningful interaction rather than another rephrasing of the same compliment. **A reward cap must not close the conversation or delete its emotional response.** The primary way to progress is discovering and acting on her preferences, followed by helping with her problems and relationship milestones. A disclosure, gift or shared experience can be useful when it serves those priorities; the owner has not named those actions as universally meaningful rewards.

Recommended preference loop: she reveals a cue through a natural reaction; the player forms an inference; a later action tests that inference; her response confirms or complicates it; appropriate relationship values change. This keeps seduction as deduction connected to the conversation. Simply choosing a different generic compliment should not count as discovering a preference.

Recommended exit presentation: show why this particular character is done through her words or actions, then settle the encounter consistently. Boredom and a boundary violation may share the result of ending the encounter while producing different emotional reactions. The owner has not yet specified the magnitude or duration of any relationship losses, repair requirements or future cooldowns.

Conversation repair includes four supporting systems previously listed as separate priorities:

- **Scene lifetime, transitions and time:** sustain genuine engagement; use personality-specific boredom and boundary exits; give transitions a clear bridge. A reward cap alone must not end the scene.
- **Character voice and personality:** author examples of welcoming, dodging and declining attention; separate clothing from speech style; choose the next reaction for the moment rather than rotate unrelated beats.
- **Recent context, memory and emotional continuity:** preserve immediate dialogue and unfinished exchanges; retrieve relevant shared moments and tensions with correct attribution; retain why a moment mattered.
- **Presentation, expressions and response processing:** compare raw and displayed replies; preserve meaningful affect cues and callbacks; avoid missing referents and expressions that contradict the reaction.

UI acceptance is about reliable, understandable play, not only visual styling. Start with the same flirting and invitation cases that exposed the conversation problem. Record what the player submits, what the control displays, and what survives save/resume. Compare button actions and typed actions where both exist. Fix only established problems; the interview's broad reliability goal does not establish that every listed flow is currently broken. No new UI runtime inspection was performed for this draft.

## What the repository evidence supports

The [earlier Ashford audit](../../artifacts/chat-audit/chat-diagnosis.md) found competing subjects inside replies, defensive banter after ordinary friendliness, a coffee scene ending while a beach plan was beginning, authored incidents claiming unearned history, internal pricing text entering scene history, and filters removing useful references. Those are historical observations and reproductions, not a fresh playtest of today's code.

Today's working files already contain important changes:

- Campus uses `conversationStyle: continuous`. The service excludes continuous scenes from the old fixed input-budget close. It is inaccurate to diagnose today's campus as simply ending every four inputs.
- `sceneTranscript` skips the shared `SUB_TURNS` set, addressing the historical pricing-history contamination.
- Speech-routing tests cover activity words inside conversation and group pronouns. These tests use mock providers; they do not prove a human session feels natural.
- Continuation directions explicitly request staying on the subject and allow replies without a final question. A calmer continuation variant and a sustained-conversation evaluation harness also exist.

Material concerns remain visible in the source inspected:

- The ordinary response contract still requests 6–9 entries and at least four spoken NPC lines. That can pressure a simple reaction into extra business. This is a plausible mechanism, not measured proof of its effect.
- Beat selection still uses a personality-derived pool and a hash/history-length choice. Very short inputs can introduce a fresh sheet topic. Selecting a valid personality trait is not equivalent to selecting the emotionally appropriate next move.
- `actor.style` still participates in temperament and tone; the earlier audit identified a character for whom that field describes clothing.
- The service still applies `dropRepeats` and `dropStock`. These may help repetition, but their final effect on callbacks and affect cues needs measurement.
- Campus still has `goodbyeEndsDay: true`. Whether that suits a particular scene is a product decision requiring play verification.

The existing [social acts](../SOCIAL-ACTS.md) and [romance tempo](../ROMANCE-TEMPO.md) documentation already describes scene caps, repeat decay, gift limits and rank ceilings. Reuse and check those mechanisms before adding another cap system. Their existence does not prove the desired independent like/lust/trust behaviour: an implementation review must trace which values each action changes, how character-specific requirements work, and whether reaching a cap preserves the conversation. These mechanisms have not been freshly tested in this interview.

## Recommended first improvement pass

**Deliver one connected encounter first.** Meet a girl, chat, flirt, notice a preference, act on it and invite her to do something later. Her response should follow her personality and independent relationship values. A concrete accepted arrangement persists; a declined or unresolved invitation does not masquerade as a confirmed plan. The encounter ends for a grounded reason, displays understandable feedback, and a later meeting or message reflects the result. Saving and resuming preserve both the scene and its consequences.

Repeat the same flow with a second girl whose priorities differ. The shared framework should yield recognizably different emotional responses and progression opportunities. This makes the two owner complaints—unclear progress and every girl feeling alike—part of the first milestone. Existing caps, plans, preferences, relationship modules and scene handling should be connected and verified before adding parallel systems.

The following diagnostic and evaluation steps support that milestone:

1. **Capture the failure across several turns.** Start with a welcomed flirt and a future invitation, then a guarded response, preference discovery and a goodbye. Record the selected action, committed state, supplied context, raw reply and displayed pages. For invitations, distinguish acceptance, rejection, alternatives and unresolved details, and check the resulting plan. This identifies whether the break occurs before generation, during writing, during settlement or during presentation.
2. **Change one source of pressure at a time.** Compare current direction against a continuation that follows the existing subject/emotional beat. Separately compare fixed entry quotas and filtering. Keep cast, state, model and generation settings matched. Do not attribute a bundled improvement to one prompt sentence.
3. **Evaluate complete interactions.** Use the existing sustained harness for 10–15 exchanges per sequence, then have the owner read or play the final displayed version. Include both short scenes and extended conversations, comparing small talk against important topics and liked actions. Include preference discovery followed by acting on the clue, a later meeting after relationship progress, high-lust/low-trust and high-trust/low-lust cases, and repeated flirting that reaches its reward cap while the dialogue continues. Also compare personality-specific boredom and boundary exits. Score appropriate reciprocity, distinct voice, continuity, action fulfillment and natural transitions; do not reward length alone.

Proposed acceptance: no unexplained action/outcome mismatches in the selected cases; no premature close that leaves an input unanswered; correct attribution of callbacks; and the owner prefers the improved whole conversation and can distinguish the characters' emotional styles. A welcomed flirt should be judged against Venus University. Initiative should follow a full reaction and arise sometimes, rather than be mandatory each turn. Important topics and liked actions should sustain the moment, and appropriate independent increases in like, lust and trust should produce a believable difference at later meetings. Repeated flirting must have bounded, diminishing rewards and eventually require meaningful progress, while a cap alone must not terminate dialogue. ## How to wire the core flow

The following is a concrete implementation proposal based on additional source inspection, not a completed repair.

### Repair interview: input and action decisions

- **Pure typing for current story interactions.** The owner currently wants no gameplay/action buttons because their present implementation makes conversation unnatural. Buttons remain a possible future design. Their earlier answer that a Flirt button should act immediately is superseded for the current interface by the typing-only choice.
- **Free response during authored scenes.** Typed input receives a free character response even when the scene has authored choices. Do not automatically select the closest button or reduce the input to its branch. The engine still checks any proposed effects and relationship boundaries.
- **Mixed actions happen in one turn.** “I tease her about her coffee addiction and buy us two coffees” should attempt both. If the purchase is unaffordable, the teasing still happens and the failed purchase is explained naturally. Individual outcomes belong to one player action; one failed component must not discard every valid component.
- **Flirting rewards creative writing that fits her.** The owner confirms the 1/2/3 tiers: 1 point for a simple flirt, 2 for creative writing she likes and 3 for creative writing she loves. These are shared totals across like, trust and lust, not the reward for each axis. Existing encounter caps and diminishing gains still apply. The model can assess her reaction and propose which axes the exchange affects, grounded in her personality, preferences and current relationship. All three axes are eligible; this does not mean every flirt raises all three. The engine validates the proposal and distributes the shared reward without multiplying it by the number of affected axes. Handling of unwelcome flirting and calibration of liked versus loved still need tuning.

### Repair interview: relationship dynamics and feedback

- **Lust has no hard ceiling.** The owner describes “chemistry/lust” as unbounded. This concerns the accumulated value, not unlimited rewards per action: encounter caps, diminishing returns and a shared reward total still apply. Confirm the mapping between the existing chemistry field and desired lust before wiring; do not silently introduce another relationship axis.
- **Lust eases toward an evolving baseline as game time passes.** The owner confirms gradual adjustment with elapsed game time, rather than a reset triggered by ending an encounter. The owner accepts character-specific baseline milestones: discovering and acting on preferences, growing closeness and welcomed physical milestones. Ordinary flirting mainly raises temporary lust. Exact milestone definitions, baseline increases and adjustment rates remain tuning decisions. Decay should preserve the earned baseline rather than erase all progress.
- **Small visual reactions during conversation, recap afterwards.** The owner wants light, small flashing hearts similar to Venus University, smiles and an end-of-conversation recap. Character dialogue and expression still need to convey the actual emotion; a positive animation must not replace a full response or imply that every stat increased. Recap contents and precise cues for negative or mixed reactions remain to be tuned. Exact numerical popups have not been requested.

### Repair interview: memory and negative outcomes

- **Remember meaningful information and use it naturally.** The owner accepts remembering discovered preferences, personal disclosures, promises, boundaries and meaningful shared moments. She may initiate a relevant follow-up, such as asking how something the player mentioned went. Callbacks should serve the present conversation rather than become mandatory recitations of past events.
- **Minor awkwardness fades when the conversation ends.** A small awkward flirt may influence the current reaction, but its transient mood or awkwardness should not carry into the next encounter. This does not establish that every already-applied relationship change must be reversed; stat settlement and transient emotion need separate handling.
- **Serious negative outcomes use capped stat losses for now.** Serious disappointments and boundary violations cause capped relationship losses, without a persistent grudge, separate unresolved grievance or repair storyline in this iteration. The owner accepts the model proposing affected axes according to the event, with engine-enforced loss caps: for example, a broken promise primarily hurts trust rather than automatically reducing every stat. The previously agreed personality-based boundary exit still applies. Richer aftermath can be added later. Retaining a character's established boundaries does not require recording each violation as a lasting grievance. Severity pricing and exact loss caps remain tuning decisions.

### Repair interview: completion and failure decisions

- **Keep completed actions when reply generation fails.** The owner accepts preserving an already completed purchase or other effect and retrying only unfinished work. A retry must not charge again, repeat relationship gains or create a duplicate invitation. The interface should show that the response is unfinished and let the player resume it against the saved outcomes.

### Repair interview: invitations and calendar decisions

- **Vague invitations remain tentative.** If the player says “We should hang out later” and she is interested, she naturally discusses the activity and timing. Preserve the tentative idea while details are unresolved; do not present it as a booked meeting.
- **Concrete spoken agreement confirms the plan.** If the player proposes “Coffee Saturday afternoon at the café?” and she agrees, automatically save the agreed plan and show a small confirmation. No additional player confirmation is required.
- **Calendar repair belongs to this connected flow.** The owner explicitly identifies the calendar as needing work. Agreement must connect to readable plans, availability, arrival and the later encounter.
- **Broad periods govern availability; exact hours govern activities within them.** Morning, afternoon and evening determine which activities are available and NPC locations. Use exact hours for activities and appointments within those periods. Period boundaries, activity durations, travel duration and how conversation advances the clock still need concrete defaults. Existing broad-period invitations need a natural way to settle the appointment hour rather than silently inventing an agreement.
- **Remind without forcing attendance.** Show a reminder; the player chooses whether to attend and may deliberately miss a meeting. Do not automatically move the player to the appointment. Exact reminder timing remains to be specified.
- **Check availability and allow character-specific rearrangement.** She checks her schedule before accepting and can suggest another time when busy. She may rearrange existing plans depending on her personality and feelings toward the player. Any agreed change must update actual availability as well as her spoken reply; affection alone should not imply unlimited availability.
- **Conversation advances time as it unfolds.** The owner accepts short exchanges consuming a little time and longer conversations consuming more, with an appointment reminder when relevant. Concrete durations and the clock rules remain to be tuned; response length alone should not determine elapsed time.
- **Lateness and missed meetings have character-specific aftermath.** She waits according to her personality, then leaves. Missing a meeting without warning can hurt trust. Messaging beforehand allows an explanation or rescheduling; her reaction depends on the circumstances. Wait durations, penalties and cancellation deadlines remain tuning decisions rather than fixed numbers.

These are design decisions, not implemented changes.

### Current risks in the source

- `GameService.act` commits the initial engine action, then `finish` generates and saves narration. Social pricing, first-scene scoring, confrontation resolution and closing can follow as separate internal turns. A completed narrative record therefore does not by itself mean the whole player action has finished settling.
- Some pricing, confrontation and closing exceptions in `finish` are caught without surfacing a failure unless they are abort errors. The player can receive successful-looking dialogue when a later effect did not apply.
- `linesFor` reconstructs presentation from the latest non-internal turn, first-scene state, active scripted beat, highlights and closing events. Its active-beat branch can take precedence over the action's own saved pages. That creates a concrete ordering risk when an action both receives a response and starts another scene.
- The current `invite` act target supplies a place/time, but the pricing handler creates a pending plan without an explicit accepted/declined/alternative decision in that branch. An invitation, positive reaction and confirmed arrangement need distinct meanings. Model output must not leave those meanings implicit.
- Calendar time currently uses Morning/Afternoon/Evening slots, while social invitations calculate a due tick and store plans in both social and story state. Social attendance detection checks whether time crosses the due tick while the player is already at the location in a scene with her. These source paths need checking against agreed arrival windows, scheduling conflicts and actual attendance; they do not establish that every calendar meeting is broken in live play. The calendar world module primarily handles weather, holidays and closures, so repairing appointments must include the social/story plan paths as well.

### Proposed sequence

1. **Give each player action one identity and one saved result.** Typed input enters the orchestration path with the submitted text, scene, target and current revision. Preserve free responses during authored scenes. Resolve mixed input into component outcomes under the same parent action, including valid social interaction when a purchase fails. Keep internal engine events attached to that parent. The result records intent, outcomes, displayed dialogue, relationship feedback, plan result and scene transition. Future buttons, if reintroduced, should use the same path.
2. **Make finishing resumable and explicit.** Record which phases have completed: initial action, generation, social settlement and any closing/queued transition. Preserve completed effects if a later phase fails; a failed phase remains recoverable and visible. Repeating a request, retrying prose or resuming after a restart must not repeat charges, stat changes or invitations. Existing command IDs and revision checks are useful foundations. Do not hold a database transaction open during model inference.
3. **Resolve social intent and invitation decisions explicitly.** A flirt describes what the player attempted and how she responded. The model proposes affected like/lust/trust axes and reaction quality; engine rules validate and price those effects with independent limits and caps. An invitation records proposed, needs-details, accepted, declined or alternative status, with target, activity, place and time where known. Only a valid concrete accepted arrangement creates a confirmed plan. Her spoken answer and the decision must agree. Connect this to a shared clock: broad periods control available activities and NPC locations, while exact hours determine appointments and activity timing. Check conflicts before agreement, persist any schedule rearrangement, remind the player without forcing attendance and resolve the actual later meeting.
4. **Make the engine's result authoritative for the reply.** Supply character priorities, relevant state, preferences, limits and predictable outcomes before generation. Validate any bundled proposals afterwards. If prose and a validated outcome conflict, record a recoverable failure rather than silently presenting success. Keep one normal scene-generation call; retain an optional interpretation call for unresolved input and a bounded repair path for exceptional invalid output. Count actual requests.
5. **Return an ordered presentation result to the UI.** Display the submitted action's reply, its readable consequence, then its departure or next scene. The UI should not independently choose between the old turn and a newly pending scene. Persist the result so reload, backlog and replay use the same pages and ordering. Expressions and progress cues should agree with the reaction; numerical reward caps must not cut off the conversation.
6. **Gate it on two contrasting characters and a complete loop.** Test typed chat → flirt → discover/act on preference → future invitation → end encounter → save/resume → later meeting. Include free input during an authored scene, a mixed tease/purchase with insufficient money, simple/liked/loved flirting, declines, alternatives, missing plan details, capped gains, a boundary exit, failed generation, failed settlement, duplicate submission and a scene triggered by the action. Use deterministic checks for state/recovery and 27b whole-conversation evaluation for personality, emotion and flow.

The first code change should establish the saved parent result, explicit finishing status and ordered presentation for the existing social paths. Then make invitation decisions explicit and connect the desired independent relationship feedback. Wider place/item invention and daily content expansion follow after this loop works.

### First repair pass after the interview

1. **Make each typed exchange reliable.** Preserve free input, resolve mixed actions, save component outcomes and deliver her complete reaction before a transition. Keep completed effects when generation fails and resume without repeating them.
2. **Connect distinct relationship responses.** Let the model propose relevant like/trust/lust axes and reaction quality; enforce shared 1/2/3 positive rewards, diminishing returns, encounter caps and capped losses in the engine. Give each girl different preferences and priorities. Keep lust unbounded, with time-based easing toward a milestone-driven baseline.
3. **Make agreed plans playable.** Connect natural invitation negotiation to saved appointments, availability and permitted schedule rearrangement. Use broad periods for activities and NPC locations, exact hours within them, time-consuming conversations, reminders, voluntary attendance and personality-based waiting/no-show reactions.
4. **Show and remember what happened.** Present light heart animations and appropriate expressions during the exchange, followed by a concise recap. Retain meaningful facts, promises and milestones for relevant follow-ups. Clear minor awkwardness at encounter end; use stat consequences rather than a persistent grievance system for serious negative outcomes in this iteration.

Verify the complete flow with two contrasting girls, including a later meeting, save/resume, a failed reply, duplicate submission, an unaffordable mixed action, a declined or unfinished invitation, a capped flirt reward, a missed meeting and a boundary exit. Use deterministic checks for reliable state and recovery, then local 27b evaluation for natural conversation and character differences. This is the agreed design scope; these repairs have not yet been implemented.
